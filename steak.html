Steak, America's Dinner



I get out of bed. Nothing more, nothing less.

Stumbling out of my room this morning seems more painful than heading to work because at least when I’m going to work, I'm going somewhere.

Keurig Coffee is the one thing I look forward to in the mornings, but as I make my trek to the kitchen it hits me, I have no more K-cups.

Just my luck.

Shuffling through the hall, I'm suddenly reminded of Chiara, my Italian ex-girlfriend. I start reminiscing about mornings with Chiara... and her espresso.

"Italians," I think to myself, "can't keep their economy going for shit, but their espresso... mhmmm... keeps me going."

Here it is; I pull the mocha pot out from the back of the kitchen drawers. I guess, technically, I'm having a Mocha, but who are you to judge...

Rummaging through my cabinets in hopes of finding coffee beans, I stumble across an old bag of espresso grounds that expired in December and think to myself, maybe there is a God; it's only March.

Setting up the mocha pot became more annoying than I expected, and despite some inconvenient hiccups, I finally get it going. Waiting for it to brew, I notice through my living room window that the world has yet to begin. I start to think; when was the last time the sun and I woke up together?

I check the fridge for some milk and realize I’m due for a trip to the store.

In the middle of weighing my needs and wants from the store, the coffee starts spurting bean water at me. It's the mocha pot telling me the coffee's now ready.

Pouring a cup assists the aroma permeate the kitchen and leads me to the window I was previously looking through. Something about the stillness, brisk air, and fresh coffee takes me to Venice. I’ve never been nor thought of going, but now I am seeing the roads starting to ripple, the cars becoming gondolas, and my dollar store mug is now a porcelain cup from a Venetian cafe. As I take the first sip, I am reminded I could not be farther away.

Dumping the cup of coffee in the sink seemed wasteful but also a civic duty.

Dressing myself is never difficult; my uniform is a nicely pressed white tee, tucked into a pair of dark blue jeans, and an old pair of Chuck Taylors. Nothing special... As I head out of the room, my grandfather's watch reminds me that even though his time has expired, the timepiece still has life to give. I grab my keys and adjust the watch to fit my wrist, readying myself to head out.

Walking down the stairs they groan as if it's still too early to be using them; I make my way out the door and into the garage. Keys in hand and clumsiness in my veins, I kick over the bike that has been in the garage for so long it looks like it was built with the house. It wants me to think it's vintage, but it's just a 40-year-old piece of crap. I've been beating it for years hoping it would break so I can get myself a new one... they built machines stubbornly durable back in the day. How something so old can still be so useful, I wonder.

As the garage door closes I briefly second-guess my decision to start my trip with two wheels rather than four, but ringing the bell eases my doubts.

Picking up speed down the road didn't last long as the glare of the rising sun is cut out by the onslaught of road and construction equipment.

When did this happen?

I take this road every day, but for some reason, the day I go for a bike ride, it's out of commission.

Holding onto the feeling that I haven’t made a mistake, I look to my left and notice an alleyway that appears to have been there the whole time, but up until now, has just presented itself to me.

I size up whether I should take the detour or if I’m willing to ride the other way down the road and add 20 extra minutes to my commute.

I look back at the alley with my instincts fully locked in.

On the one hand, this gloomy route is filled with dumpsters, darkness, and despair; on the other hand, shortcut.

Taking the latter made me question my morning decisions. As I passed up the forgotten parts of the city, I make it out the other end before the smell and texture of my surroundings could get to me. Regardless of the experience, making it through that alley saved me at least twenty minutes, and by the sounds of my heavy breathing from the mile I’d ridden so far, it was well worth it.

Its time-saving abilities came at a cost, as the alley shoots me out into a part of the city that I am not immediately familiar with. I step off the bike to get my bearings. Looking around for an address, I notice this rustic wooden sign swinging from a cast iron rod with a rat holding two meat cleavers and a chef’s hat. Charming. Although the paint was chipping, I could make out the name in the ribbon carved below the rat; it read, “Ol Rat’s Butcher Shop.”

Intrigued, I lean the bike on the alleyway wall with the confidence that anyone willing to steal it must need that old bike more than I do. Walking up to the window, for some reason, I couldn't get over how clean the glass was. I catch myself in the reflection and think to myself "Damn, there's no better outfit than jeans and a white tee. Being a handsome motherfucker sure helps too but it even makes the ugly ones look good".

Behind my handsome reflection, I notice steaks being presented so meticulously it makes me feel the need to clean my room. Beyond the display cases, behind the counter, I see an older gentleman, who I’m sure is the butcher, getting prepped for his day. The man moved slow, even for his age, but with purpose. There was no wasted movement and in those few moments, I could tell that he had been doing this for quite some time. While studying the butcher, I thought, “When was the last time I even had a steak?” I chuckled as I reminisced about my college roommates and I making “Dorm Steaks” flattened-out ground beef that we had to microwave because hot plates weren’t allowed in our room. As if the butcher could sense me daydreaming, he suddenly stopped and quickly peered over his shoulder, looking at me so intensely that it made me jump.

Sizing me up as I had done to him, he waives me inside despite the sign on the glass door saying otherwise.

The bell chimes as I walk through the door. The butcher, without looking, dismissively says, “I’ll get to you in a sec.”

I see the charm doesn't end with the sign.

Instead of twiddling my thumbs, I looked around the shop and noticed the right wall. It is filled with distant memories and accomplishments of the butcher shop. In the pictures, I see an aspiring young man resembling the one behind the counter, but the ambitious glisten in the young man’s eyes told me that although they appeared to be the same man, there was no doubt in my mind that they were different people. An article caught my eye that read, “Art ‘The Rat’ Has the Best Cuts in The City!” as I start to read the small lettering next to a picture of the young man holding a plaque above his head in front of the butcher shop, I hear—

“Nice watch.”

Startled, I turn and say, “Thanks, my grandfather gave it to me.”

“Huh... so whatcha need?”

Such a simple question that I wasn't prepared to answer.

“Um, steak.”

I knew it was a stupid response as soon as the words left my mouth, and the butcher let me know it.

“No shit.”

Sensing my embarrassment as I looked to the ground the butcher compassionately rolled his eyes, shook his head, and said, “Alright, what kinda steak do you normally get?”

Looking around the shop to get inspiration from the organized meat-case I spew out the only two cuts I managed to scrape from my memory.

“Filet mignon, but I have been wanting to try a tomahawk.”

“Tomahawk... that was good. He’ll definitely appreciate that,” I think to myself.

“You kids and that fucking tomahawk.”

I was wrong.

“You know it's just an expensive ribeye with a handle?”

Sparing me from any more embarrassment, the butcher asks, “Alright, you ever heard of a Delmonico?”

Of course, I haven’t.

“I think so.”

The butcher chuckles and explains, “I get my stuff from a rancher bout two hours north who tends to leave a little extra meat on the bone.”

Butcher puns... hilarious.

“I call it the Brooklyn,” he smugs, “but that’s just because I like to cut it a little different. They usually only give me a few to enjoy and not sell, but I just so happen to have an extra one if you’re interested.”

Before I could even get a thank you out, the butcher had already turned and started wrapping the steak with the same precision I admired from the window.

“What do I owe you?” I stammer.

“Don’t worry about it kid. It’s on the house,” the butcher responds as he holds out the freshly wrapped steak that's reminiscent of a Christmas present from the 1930s.

“Wow thanks.”

“Don’t mention it.”

As I reach for the steak, I am met with slight resistance. A chill comes over me, thinking that the butcher realized he's made a mistake. I look up, and my eyes are met with the same glisten I noticed from the young man in the pictures on the wall.

“Medium-high pan,

Butter,

Rosemary,

Five minutes each side,

Let it rest for ten.”

I nod appreciatively and make my way to the door.

“Aye kid!” the butcher bellows from across the shop, “How are you going to cook that steak?” in a tone testing me.

Peering over my shoulder with a smirk I bellow back

“I’m gunna fucking microwave it.”
